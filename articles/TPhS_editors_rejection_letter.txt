Dear Colleague,
I am writing to you in my capacity as editor of Transactions of the Philological Society. Thank you for your submission entitled ‘Necromancing Diels: computerising the phonological analysis of early Slavonic texts using existing treebank data and a Late Common Slavonic computerised inflectional morphology’. I apologise for taking a long time to get back to you with an outcome: it was extremely difficult to find suitably qualified reviewers that were also available.
I have now received both reviews of your submission, I should note that one of the two reviewers sent their comments by email, and I attach them to this letter (see the link called Editorial Comments).
I have discussed the reviewers’ evaluations with the consulting editors, and I am afraid that there is consensus that your manuscript cannot be accepted for publication in Transactions. I am sorry to be the bearer of bad news.
As you will see, while both reviewers are appreciative of your work, they also independently recommend that you should submit to another, more suitable, journal. The key problem is that the article does not include an original linguistic study, which is not what is expected of work published in TPhS. That said, we do welcome the suggestion of one reviewer that if you decided to use your digital tool to conduct a novel study, we should consider it as an entirely new submission. If you decided to do this, we would encourage you to make your article accessible to a general linguistic or philological readership rather than address it solely to specialists (see our style sheet here: https://philsoc.org.uk/transactions).
We hope that the reviewers’ comments will help you enhance the quality of your paper before submitting it to another journal and that your work will eventually find a good home.

With very best wishes,
Professor Delia Bentley, on behalf of the editorial team
